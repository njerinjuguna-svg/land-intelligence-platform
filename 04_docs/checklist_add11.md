| B7 | **Piped water and sewer networks — WASREB Majidata.** `dist_water_line_m` and `dist_sewer_m` have existed in the schema since v1.0 and have never held a value, and there is **no free path to filling them**: mains are buried, so OpenStreetMap has essentially nothing, and loading its scraps would reproduce exactly the failure already noted for power lines, where a large distance means "nobody mapped it" rather than "there is no pipe". The real source exists and is the regulator's own. **Majidata** is Kenya's national georeferenced water and sanitation information system, mandated under **Section 111 of the Water Act 2016**, and by its own description holds spatial data for the distribution and sewer networks of licensed Water Service Providers plus each WSP's licensed service-area boundary. **ASK:** access terms for (a) the WSP service-area polygons and (b) the distribution/sewer network geometry, and whether commercial use in a paid product is permitted. **Ask for the service areas first** — "this plot is inside Thika Water's licensed area" answers *can I get a connection at all*, is a coarse polygon rather than a pipe network, and is far less sensitive for a utility to release. Bundle with the KWS and KPLC letters; same template. Note also that the 2019 census publishes households by main water source down to sub-county level, which answers a DIFFERENT question — an area percentage, handled like `coverage_4g_pct` under D4, never dressed as a distance. | WASREB — info@wasreb.go.ke |

| D23 | **NEVER PUBLISH A LANDMARK A BUYER CANNOT USE.** D20 says do not display a field we do not source. This is its harder half: **a field we DO source, measured correctly, can still be the wrong answer.** Two cases, one session. `Shekiko Airport (disused)` was TEST-TANADELTA-01's nearest airport at 7.5 km — out of service, and OSM said so in the name, so `etl_30`'s `DEFUNCT` filter catches it and everything like it. `GSU Airstrip` was the nearest airport on **all eight OAK GROVE plots** at 6.9 km — fully operational, a police airstrip, and **nothing in the name, the geometry or the tags says a buyer cannot fly from it**. No filter can find that, because the defect is not in the data; it is in the question. Fixed by adding `dist_intl_airport_m` / `nearest_intl_airport_name`: `international` is the ONLY aviation class this database verifies rather than infers (four facilities, each named so by its operator), and it is what buyers see. **`dist_airport_m` must be labelled "airstrip" on any page, never "airport"** — the difference is the entire point. The same rule blocked villages from answering `dist_town_centre_m`: 8,679 of 9,323 places are villages, and measuring to the nearest of any kind would have measured OSM's tagging density rather than access to a town. |

| D24 | **THE AVAILABILITY PANEL IS A SECOND ONBOARDING HANDSHAKE, AND IT IS ONGOING.** The scheme-availability view — *"3 of 7 plots still available"* — is the strongest sales feature on the buyer page, and it is the only one that depends on the client doing something **after** go-live. Today all eight OAK GROVE plots read `listing_status = 'available'` and every `price_kes` is NULL, so the panel has nothing to show. The `parcel_ref` mapping is agreed once at onboarding and then holds; **listing status has to be maintained, or the panel silently becomes a stale page telling buyers that sold plots are for sale** — which is worse than not showing it. Put it in the agreement: who updates status, through what (feed, API, or their admin screen), and how often. Then have the widget hide the panel entirely when the data is older than an agreed threshold, rather than displaying it confidently. |

| E14 | **A green verification does not mean the answers are right — read them out loud.** `verify_01` and `verify_02` were both fully green when four PDFs were read end to end and every one carried a defect no assertion would ever catch: PLOT-950 refused to score because the boundary was untrusted and then asserted "on the plot itself: river" four lines lower (containment is made ENTIRELY out of the boundary; a distance survives a bad one and containment does not); TEST-KANO-01 printed "89 / 100 — Good" on a plot the model caps at 35 for building; Athi Plains led its amenity list with a river 161 m away, ranked first because the list sorts by distance; and a disused airstrip and a police airstrip were each served as "your nearest airport". **In every case the query was right, the join was right, the distance was right to the metre, and the column was answering the question NEXT TO the one a buyer was asking.** A filter can catch a fact the source admits about itself. It cannot catch a question that was subtly wrong. **Budget time each session to read the actual output — not the test summary — on at least one complete parcel.** |

| E15 | **`transport.bus_stops` holds every public-transport stop, not just buses — RENAME IT.** After `etl_30` the table contains bus stops, bus stations, matatu/taxi stages, railway stations, railway halts and ferry terminals, distinguished only by `stop_type`. The name is narrower than the contents, and `layer_landmarks` already has to explain in a comment why it queries a table called `bus_stops` for railway stations. This is E13's shape one level up: **a NAME that lies is read later as a description of what is there.** It was left alone deliberately rather than renamed quietly — a rename touches the ETL, the engine and the verifier at once, and doing it mid-session would have mixed a cosmetic change into a functional one. Do it as its own change, with the snapshot diff of E10 as the check. Suggested: `transport.transit_stops`. |
