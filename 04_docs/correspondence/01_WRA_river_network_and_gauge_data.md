**RE: REQUEST FOR SURFACE WATER NETWORK DATA AND HYDROLOGICAL RECORDS**

Geocode Spatial Solutions Ltd is a Kenyan geospatial company building a
national land intelligence database. The platform assesses land parcels against
physical and regulatory factors — terrain, soils, rainfall, flood exposure,
proximity to infrastructure, and statutory setbacks — and produces a written
assessment for land buyers and land-selling companies.

This is a commercial product, and we are approaching you at the point where
that matters, rather than after the fact.

Two of the factors we assess fall directly within your mandate, and we would
rather build them on your data than on approximations. We respectfully request
the following.

**1. The surface water network (highest priority)**

The digitised river and stream network for Kenya, in a GIS format
(shapefile or geodatabase), including where available:

- the watercourse name;
- the classification of each reach as **perennial or seasonal**;
- any classification of channel type, distinguishing natural watercourses from
  canals and artificial drains.

The perennial/seasonal distinction is the attribute we most need and cannot
derive. We currently hold a watercourse network from an open mapping source
that carries no such classification, so every reach is treated identically —
which is plainly wrong for a country where a great many channels are seasonal.

**2. Licence terms for commercial use**

We would like written confirmation of the terms on which the above may be used
in a commercial product, including any attribution wording you require and any
restriction on onward redistribution. We do not seek to redistribute your data.
Our need is to compute derived measurements from it — for example "the nearest
watercourse is 340 metres away" — and to publish those measurements, not the
network itself.

If a licence fee or data access agreement applies, we would be glad to be
advised of it.

**3. Streamflow gauge records, for flood model calibration**

We have built a national flood susceptibility model using terrain analysis
(Height Above Nearest Drainage, derived from Copernicus 30-metre elevation
data). It scales the expected flood depth by the size of the catchment draining
to each point, using a depth–area relationship taken from the general
hydrological literature rather than from Kenyan observation.

That single borrowed constant is the weakest element in the model. Access to
historical peak discharge or stage records from your gauging stations — even a
modest sample across contrasting basins such as the Nzoia, Tana and Ewaso
Ng'iro — would let us fit it to Kenyan conditions.

**What we would offer in return**

We recognise this is a substantial request and would like it to be reciprocal.
We would be pleased to:

- share our national flood susceptibility layer with the Authority at no cost,
  together with its full methodology, for whatever internal use you judge
  appropriate;
- credit the Authority explicitly as the source of the water network in every
  report where it is used;
- provide our riparian buffer analysis, which applies the statutory setbacks
  under the Environmental Management and Co-ordination (Wetlands, Riverbanks,
  Lakeshores and Seashores) Regulations 2009 and the Water (Resources)
  Regulations 2025 to the national network. This identifies, parcel by parcel,
  where land being offered for sale overlaps a riparian reserve.

We would note on that last point that a measurable amount of land currently
being marketed to buyers in Kenya sits within riparian reserves. Buyers are
generally not told. Making that visible at the point of purchase is one of the
purposes of our platform, and it is an interest we take to be shared with the
Authority.

We would welcome a meeting at your offices to discuss any of the above, and are
happy to work through whatever formal application process you require.

## Notes on why it is written this way

**The three requests are ordered by what we most need and by what is easiest
to grant.** The river network is a dataset that already exists; the gauge
records may require more internal approval. Putting the hard ask last means a
partial yes is still a useful yes.

**The reciprocity is real, not decoration.** A national flood layer and a
riparian encroachment analysis are genuinely useful to a water regulator. This
is a fair exchange rather than a favour, and it should be presented as one.

**The riparian point is the strongest card and is placed near the end.** It
aligns our commercial interest with their regulatory mandate: we both want
buyers to know when land carries a statutory reserve. Overstating it would look
like flattery, so it is stated once, factually.

**We say plainly that we do not seek to redistribute.** This is the single
sentence most likely to make the licence easy to grant. A derived-measurement
licence is far simpler to approve internally than a data release — the same
reasoning behind the Kenya Power approach.
