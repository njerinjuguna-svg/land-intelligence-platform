product rules that were discovered while building and must not be lost.

| # | Item | Why it matters |

| C1 | **RESOLVED, session 7. `AMIN_TOP_KM2` stays at 10.** | Re-run on the clipped denominator (69M Kenyan cells vs 117M in the box). **There is no headroom to lower it, for a structural reason:** `CHANNEL_KM2 = 5`, so every outlet catchment is ≥ 5 km² by construction and any floor ≤ 5 cannot bind — 0/3/5 return identical rows by identity, not measurement. ## D. PRODUCT RULES — must be enforced in the enrichment engine and reports

These are not caveats to print in small type. They change what the software
is allowed to say.

| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| D1 | **Flood is MODELLED, not measured.** Every buyer-facing use must say so, and must not be presented as a substitute for a site visit or hydrological study. |
| D2 | **REWRITTEN, session 7 — the original wording was implemented exactly and produced a wrong answer on 11 of 12 parcels.** "Worst class within 1 km" as a maximum returned `very_high` for almost every parcel in Kenya, including Karen, because channel cells sit at HAND=0 and classify Very high (C10) and there is a channel within 1 km of almost anywhere. A neighbourhood *share* cannot fix it either — Karen's buffer is 4.31% `very_high`, Garissa's is 4.48%. **The rule is now: `flood_risk_class` is the worst class covering a MATERIAL SHARE OF THE PARCEL (≥10%), never the neighbourhood maximum and never the dominant class.** Garissa's own footprint is 25% `very_high` while its dominant class is `low` — the dominant class buries a quarter of the parcel, the neighbourhood maximum flags the country. The neighbourhood is still reported, as **context against the national baseline** (`flood_nearby_pct` vs 12.5% of Kenya in High or Very high), never as the headline. The original intent stands: a buyer needs to know what is next door. |
| D3 | **Flood class 6 = permanent water is a LAND-COVER FACT, not a hazard level.** Never render it as "beyond very high". But it is **not benign** — proximity to permanent water is its own risk signal and must be surfaced (this is what carries Budalangi's warning). |
| D4 | **Mobile coverage is a SUBLOCATION percentage.** Say "the area around this parcel is about 65% 4G-covered", **never** "this parcel has 4G". Pair with distance-to-nearest-tower, which IS point-specific. |
| D5 | **Absence of Airtel coverage is absence of DATA, not absence of coverage.** The CA did not publish an Airtel percentage. Any per-operator display must say so explicitly. |
| D6 | **Never tell a buyer "this is not farmland" from land cover alone.** WorldCover under-detects smallholder mosaic farming; NDVI showed up to 4.2× the mapped cropland area is as green as cropland. Use NDVI + rainfall + soil together. |
| D7 | **PARTIALLY IMPLEMENTED — taxonomy arm only. See C16 before using this column at all.** The rule was right about the failure (SoilGrids misses the Athi-Kapiti plains, exactly where Nairobi peri-urban selling is most active) and wrong about the remedy: **iSDA clay texture cannot supply the missing arm.** Measured on the test parcels, texture and slope both point the wrong way between Athi and Karen. The engine reports the Vertisols arm and carries the miss as a named gap. **Where `black_cotton_risk` reads `none` on flat peri-urban land around Nairobi, the report must say INCONCLUSIVE, never "safe".** |
| D8 | **Built-up: a full cell is 8,606 m², and the fraction must be capped at 1.0.** Not 8,548. GHSL is computed on Mollweide and regridded; the wrong divisor makes a parcel read "101% built". |
| D9 | **Population: prefer relative comparisons** ("more people near A than B") over absolute counts, until KNBS census is loaded. |
| D10 | **Nightlights `trend_radiance_yr` is a SUM and scales with unit area.** Never compare a large ward to a small one on it directly. |
| D11 | **`trend_pct_yr` is deliberately NULL.** A percentage rate from a near-zero base ranked rural electrification above the peri-urban land market. Do not repopulate it as a development signal. |
| D12 | **Coverage vintages span Jan 2022 – Aug 2023 and are not comparable across technologies.** 2G covers 9,274 polygons, 4G covers 7,134. Per-parcel lookup is fine; national cross-technology comparison is not. |
| D13 | **Nightlight radiance is not linear in economic activity.** VIIRS compresses bright cores. SNPP's sensor also degraded across the series. |
| D14 | **Every composition percentage must be shown with its sample size, or not shown.** Karen is 8,093 m² sampled at 30 m: **nine pixels.** "33.3% Clay" from nine pixels is three pixels. The engine writes `n_pixels` and a `SMALL SAMPLE` warning into `field_sources` for every composition; the report must honour it. A percentage without its n invites exactly the false confidence this product is sold to prevent. |
| D15 | **Duplicate or overlapping parcel geometry is REJECTED at upload, never enriched.** PLOT-1069 and PLOT-1070 arrived `ST_Equals` — two plot numbers, one polygon. In this market that is either a drafting duplicate or **the same ground allocated twice**, and a platform that enriches both bills for the error instead of catching it. Adjacent plots sharing a boundary are normal subdivision layout and are not a finding; real overlap is. Likewise a parcel **repaired** at load (C17): flag it, hold it, do not quote it. |
| D16 | **Never build parcel polygons from a beacon list.** `THIKA ALL.csv` is 1,715 survey beacons with no topology — a1, a6 and a5 are collinear to within a decimetre, so consecutive points are boundary runs, not plot corners. Reconstructing polygons from it would be inventing geometry and then selling analysis computed on it. Plot topology comes from the client as a DXF, a shapefile or the mutation drawing, or the parcel is not enriched. |
| D17 | **DO NOT SHIP PER-OPERATOR 4G AT ALL — not one column, not both.** `coverage_4g_pct_safaricom` and `coverage_4g_pct_telkom` are the same measurements under two operator labels (B1b). Dropping one does not fix it: we do not know which operator the surviving column describes. **The combined layer (`operator='all'`) is unaffected and is what a report quotes.** Lift this only on a written answer from the CA. |
| D18 | **`dist_* = 0` IS CONTAINMENT, NOT A SMALL DISTANCE — never render it as "0 m".** The feature lies *on* the parcel, which is a different kind of fact from "nearby" and reads in a report as a rounding artefact or a missing value. It matters most where it matters most: `dist_river_m = 0` means **a watercourse crosses the plot** (riparian reserve, constrained build); `dist_protected_area_m = 0` means the parcel **intersects a gazetted area**, which is a stop-work finding and not a proximity score. For `dist_tower_m`, containment at confidence 2 on crowd-estimated positions means "very close", never "the mast is on this land". The engine writes an `INSIDE_THE_PARCEL` note on every such field. |
| D19 | **A coverage percentage below 0.01% is a sliver artefact, not a measurement.** Telkom 4G returned `6.8e-07%` and `3.5e-07%` on two parcels — the centroid clipping a hairline overlap between adjacent polygons. "Telkom coverage: 0.0000007%" states an operator percentage that was never measured. The engine refuses these and keeps the raw value in `field_sources`. |
| D20 | **NEVER DISPLAY A FIELD THE PLATFORM DOES NOT SOURCE.** The buyer-facing design asked for four Key Insights we cannot support: **Title Status: Verified** (we hold no title data at all), **Electricity Access: Yes (200 m)** (KPLC blocked on A5 — the open map we hold misses most low-voltage lines), **Land Use Zone: Residential** (`land.zoning` is empty), and **5G** (does not exist in our data; 4G does, and only as a sublocation percentage per D4). Each shows as **"Not yet checked"** with what it is waiting on. A blank labelled honestly is a product — it makes the roadmap visible and turns "your advocate must do the search" into a trust signal. **A plausible-looking value is a liability**, and on title specifically it is the claim most likely to end in court. |
| D21 | **A blocking finding is never rendered as a low score.** Land inside a protected area, or a parcel whose boundary is not trusted, returns NULL scores and a stated reason — never 12/100. A low number invites a comparison ("12 is worse than 15") that must not be made about land which is not for sale or ground we cannot locate. It will be tempting to "improve" the model later by giving blocked parcels a floor value; that is a regression wearing the clothes of a fix, and `verify_02_suitability.py` section A exists to catch it. |
| D22 | **Some inputs are CEILINGS, not contributions.** TEST-LODWAR-01 first scored 54 residential on a **very high** flood class, because flood carried 0.28 of the weight and good road access earned the rest back. Raising the weight would have been the E6 mistake — tuning a constant when the model is wrong. A buyer does not trade flood risk against mobile coverage; past a point the hazard decides. Caps are applied, and the uncapped figure is always kept in the breakdown so the cap can be argued with rather than discovered. |

## E. STANDING ENGINEERING RULES (learned the hard way)

| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| E1 | **Clip to the country before quoting any national statistic.** **FIVE occurrences now**, not three: land cover (s5), flood twice (runs 62, 75), rainfall (run 78), IO land cover (run 81) — and the last two happened *after* this rule was written down, the rainfall one within an hour of writing it. Sanity check: does the mask measure ~580,367 km²? **A rule in a checklist is not a control.** etl_24, etl_27, etl_28, etl_29 and `calibrate_scaled_hazard.py` now each print the box and Kenya denominators side by side and *refuse to run* if the mask is out. **Still to do: extract this into one shared helper every raster ETL imports, so clipping is the default and skipping it takes effort.** |
| E8 | **Catch `BaseException`, not `Exception`, in any handler that closes a run row.** `SystemExit` (every `raise SystemExit` guard — 96 across 10 files) and `KeyboardInterrupt` inherit from `BaseException` and sail past `except Exception`. The result is backwards: a script that crashes on an unanticipated bug logs correctly, while one stopped by a guard it deliberately wrote does not. **Cost: 12 rows sat at `running` for up to a month while PROGRESS.md recorded them as failures.** Fixed by `03_etl/patch_etl_failure_logging.py`; reconciled by `01_database/08_reconcile_orphan_etl_runs.sql`. |
| E10 | **Snapshot the schema after any change, and diff it.** `pg_dump -U postgres -d land_intelligence_kenya --schema-only --no-owner --no-privileges -f 01_database/_live_schema_snapshot.sql`, then compare against the previous snapshot. **The database could not be rebuilt from its own SQL files** and nobody knew: `02_schema_update_v1.1.sql` had been overwritten with a scratch SELECT, taking with it six sales statuses, two columns, a trigger, an index and seven composition/range columns. Reconstructed in session 7 from the live database. Ten seconds a session is the whole cost, and it is the only thing that would have caught it. **BLOCKING BEFORE THE CLOUD MIGRATION:** diff the full snapshot against the versioned files — session 7 only checked the three tables the trail led to. |
| E9 | **A control a pipeline can overwrite is not a control.** `landcover_change` was quarantined by SQL, then re-catalogued as `active` by the next successful etl_29 run — inside its own final transaction. The quarantine now lives in etl_29 itself. Same shape as E1 and E8: **if the rule does not live in the code's own failure path, it is a reminder.** |
| E2 | **Query it, don't recall it.** Six values were asserted from memory in session 6 and every one was wrong: `metadata.sources` columns, `admin.counties.name`, county name aliases, `slcode` semantics, EOG's OAuth secret, the LAADS ArchiveSet. Each cost a failed run; each was settled in seconds by reading the source. |
| E3 | **Print the background distribution BEFORE setting any threshold.** |
| E4 | **A verification that prints the model's own output is not a test.** Name the expected answers in the script before choosing the metric. |
| E5 | **A size check is not an integrity check.** Verify content (does it open? does the byte count match Content-Length?), never just "bigger than zero". |
| E6 | **If you have changed the same constant twice, stop changing it.** Sweep it. If no value satisfies the constraints, the model is wrong, not the constant. |
| E7 | **Separate FETCH from everything downstream.** Cache raw downloads; a dropped connection should cost one page, not a whole layer. |
| E11 | **One `metadata.raster_catalog.variable` must identify ONE raster.** `etl_26_nightlights.py` writes a row per year, all under `variable = 'nightlights'`. The enrichment engine keeps the newest `raster_id` per variable, so `cat['nightlights']` silently resolves to *whichever year was catalogued last* — a choice nothing states and nothing tests. `etl_29` does it right: `landcover_io_2017`, `landcover_io_2023`, `landcover_io_2024`. **Until etl_26 is fixed, nightlights must be read from `demographics.nightlights_stats`, not from the raster catalogue.** |
| E12 | **A metric that returns a plausible number whether or not the thing is broken is not evidence.** E4 says name the expected answer before choosing the metric; the sharper form is **name what the metric would read if the thing were BROKEN, and check that it differs from what it reads if the thing is fine.** Three CRS/geometry tests failed this in one session: a centroid check returning an identical 272 m for datums 315 m apart, a nearest-beacon check that was measuring point density (one point per ~27 m in a cloud of 1,715), and a containment check that cannot pass because survey beacons sit ON boundaries and `ST_Contains` excludes them. |
| E13 | **A constant with a justifying comment and no reference is a lie in waiting.** `NEARBY_MIN_PCT` and `FLAT_SLOPE_DEG` in `enrich_01_engine.py` each carry several lines explaining a control, and neither is used anywhere in the file — they are fossils of two abandoned approaches. Wire it in or delete it. A comment describing a control that does not exist is worse than no comment, because it will be read later as a description of what the code does. |
